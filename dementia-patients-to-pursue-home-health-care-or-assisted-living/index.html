Dementia: home health care or assisted living?

Weighing safety, comfort, routine and cost when someone you love is living with dementia.

By the Nikko Homecare nursing team · 2 November 2026 · 7 min read

When someone you love is living with dementia, one of the biggest questions families face is where they should be cared for. Should they stay at home with support, or move to a residence with care around the clock? There is no single right answer, but understanding the options makes the decision clearer.

Why familiarity matters

People living with dementia often feel safest in familiar surroundings. Their own home, routines, belongings and neighbourhood can help reduce confusion and distress. For many people in the early and middle stages, care at home with the right support allows them to stay connected to the life they know.

When home care works well

  • Your loved one is generally safe at home with some supervision.
  • Family members are nearby and able to share the caring role.
  • Care needs are mainly personal care, meals, medication and companionship.
  • Their home can be adapted to reduce risks such as falls or wandering.

When a residence may be safer

  • Your loved one needs supervision day and night, or is at risk of wandering.
  • There have been repeated falls, hospital stays or sudden changes in health.
  • Family caregivers are exhausted, or live too far away to help every day.
  • Medical needs have grown beyond what can be managed safely at home.

Thinking about cost

Costs depend on how much care is needed. A few hours of support a week at home is very different from live-in or round-the-clock nursing. A residence brings nursing, meals and accommodation together. A care assessment helps you compare realistic options for your situation.

It doesn’t have to be one or the other

Many families combine both. Care at home for as long as it is safe, with respite stays at a residence to give family caregivers a break, or a move to residential care when needs change. Plans can and should change as your loved one’s needs do.

Getting advice

Every person with dementia is different. Speaking to their doctor and to an experienced nurse can help you understand their needs today and plan for the future. We’re happy to talk it through with you.
