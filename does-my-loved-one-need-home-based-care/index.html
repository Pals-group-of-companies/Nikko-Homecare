Does my loved one need home-based care?

The signs to look for, what home care can include, and how to start the conversation with your family.

By the Nikko Homecare nursing team · 2 November 2026 · 6 min read

Most families don’t decide on home care in a single moment. It usually builds slowly: a fall that could have been worse, a fridge that’s emptier than it should be, a parent who seems tired of managing alone. If you’re wondering whether it’s time, you’re probably already noticing something.

Signs it may be time for support

Every situation is different, but these are some of the most common signs that extra help at home would make a difference:

  • Daily tasks such as bathing, dressing or cooking are becoming difficult or unsafe.
  • Medication is being missed, doubled up or taken at the wrong times.
  • There have been falls, near-falls or new trouble getting up from a chair or bed.
  • Weight loss, poor appetite or signs that meals are being skipped.
  • Unopened post, unpaid bills or growing forgetfulness.
  • A recent hospital stay, surgery or new diagnosis.
  • You, or another family caregiver, are exhausted and stretched too thin.

What home-based care can include

Home care isn’t one fixed service. It can be a caregiver for a few hours a week, or nursing support around the clock. Depending on your loved one’s needs, care might cover personal care and hygiene, meals and medication reminders, wound care and monitoring by a nurse, physiotherapy, and simply company and conversation.

Starting the conversation

Talking about care can feel difficult, especially with a parent who values their independence. It often helps to focus on what they want to keep doing, rather than what they can no longer do. Involve them in every decision, start with a small amount of help, and remember that the goal is to help them stay at home for longer.

Home care or a residence?

For many people, staying at home with the right support is the best option. For others, particularly after a hospital stay or when needs change quickly, a short or longer stay at a residence with nurses on duty 24/7 can be safer. There is no wrong answer, only what works best for your family right now.

The next step

If you recognise some of these signs, a conversation with a nurse can help you understand the options. We’ll listen to what’s happening, assess your loved one’s needs, and suggest a care plan that fits.
